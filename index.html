A Field Guide to Other Worlds

Every Dot a World

Most of the stars you can see at night have planets. This guide invents one at a time, using the same physics astronomers use to make sense of the 6,000-plus real exoplanets, and shows you what it would be like and whether we could ever see it.

How to use this guide

  • New planet builds a fresh world. Every world has its own link, so you can share it or come back to it.
  • Drag the planet to look around it. The tabs on the right turn to each page: the view from its surface, its star, orbit, interior, air and more.
  • Why these values? on each page explains the reasoning and the research behind every number.
  • Move the distance slider on the Observe page to see which telescopes, today and in the coming decades, could detect it.
  • Interesting favours rarer and more habitable worlds; Realistic shows what a typical planet is like.
  • Technical key shows every value, unit and note up front, and lets you change the model's inputs.
  • How it works is a flowchart of the whole model, from the seed to the finished planet, traced step by step for the world you're looking at.
